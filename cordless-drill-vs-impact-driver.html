Cordless Drill vs Impact Driver: What's the Difference?

Cordless drills and impact drivers may look similar, but they are designed for different jobs. Understanding the difference can help you choose the right tool for outdoor projects, repairs and DIY work.

Quick Summary
  • Drills are more versatile for general drilling and fastening.
  • Impact drivers provide high rotational force for driving screws.
  • A drill usually offers better control for delicate tasks.
  • An impact driver is useful for long screws and tougher fastening jobs.
  • For general household use, a cordless drill may be the better first tool.
  • Heavy DIY users may benefit from owning both.

What Is a Cordless Drill?

A cordless drill is a versatile power tool designed primarily for drilling holes and driving screws.

Most cordless drills use a chuck that can accept different drill bits and driver bits. This makes them useful for a wide variety of household, workshop and outdoor projects.

What Is an Impact Driver?

An impact driver is designed primarily for driving screws, bolts and other fasteners that require more rotational force.

Instead of relying only on continuous motor torque, an impact driver uses a hammering mechanism that delivers additional rotational force when resistance increases.

1. When Should You Use a Cordless Drill?

A cordless drill is usually the better choice when you need one tool that can handle many different tasks.

Common uses include:

  • Drilling holes in wood
  • Drilling into some plastics and metals
  • Driving small and medium screws
  • Installing shelves
  • Assembling furniture
  • Building small outdoor projects
  • Installing brackets and hardware

2. When Should You Use an Impact Driver?

An impact driver becomes especially useful when a fastening job requires more torque.

It can be useful for:

  • Driving long construction screws
  • Building decks
  • Outdoor construction projects
  • Installing heavy-duty hardware
  • Working with larger fasteners

3. Which Tool Is Easier to Control?

A cordless drill generally provides more control because it usually has an adjustable clutch.

The clutch allows the user to control how much torque is applied before the tool stops driving.

This can be useful when working with smaller screws or materials that can easily be damaged by excessive force.

4. Which Tool Is Better for Long Screws?

An impact driver is generally better suited to driving long screws into dense materials.

Its impact mechanism can provide additional rotational force when the screw encounters resistance.

This makes impact drivers particularly useful for construction and outdoor building projects.

5. Can an Impact Driver Drill Holes?

An impact driver can be used for some drilling tasks when paired with an appropriate hex-shank drill bit.

However, it is not usually as versatile as a conventional drill. If drilling is a major part of your work, a cordless drill is generally the more practical choice.

6. What About Outdoor Projects?

Both tools can be useful outdoors.

Garden Projects

A cordless drill can be useful for building raised beds, assembling garden structures, installing hooks and making small repairs.

Decks and Larger Structures

An impact driver can be more convenient when driving many long screws into wood.

General Outdoor Maintenance

If your projects vary widely, a cordless drill may be the better single-tool solution.

7. Battery Platform Matters

If you are buying cordless tools, consider the battery platform before choosing a brand.

Staying within one battery system can allow you to use the same batteries across multiple compatible tools.

This can reduce the need to buy separate batteries and chargers for every tool.

8. Should You Buy Both?

If you frequently work on DIY or construction projects, owning both tools can be useful.

The drill can handle general drilling and precision fastening, while the impact driver can handle tougher fastening jobs.

However, beginners do not necessarily need both immediately. Starting with the tool that matches your most common tasks can keep the setup simpler and less expensive.

Buying Checklist

  • ✔ Choose a cordless drill if versatility is your priority.
  • ✔ Choose an impact driver for frequent heavy fastening.
  • ✔ Consider the types of materials you normally work with.
  • ✔ Check the battery platform before buying cordless tools.
  • ✔ Look for variable speed control.
  • ✔ Consider the tool's weight and balance.
  • ✔ Think about whether you actually need both tools.

Which One Should You Buy First?

For most homeowners and beginners, a cordless drill is usually the better first purchase because it can handle a wider variety of tasks.

If your main projects involve construction, decking or repeatedly driving long screws, an impact driver may make more sense.

The best choice depends on the work you actually plan to do rather than simply choosing the more powerful tool.

Final Thoughts

Cordless drills and impact drivers are complementary tools rather than direct replacements for one another.

A drill is generally more versatile, while an impact driver excels at demanding fastening tasks.

If you are building your first cordless tool collection, start with the tool that matches your most common projects and add the other later if your needs grow.
